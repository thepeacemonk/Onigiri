Understanding Your Metrics

🎯 Retention Score

What it measures: The percentage of mature card reviews (cards with an interval of 21+ days) that you answered correctly (ease 2 or higher).

How it's calculated: Correct mature reviews divided by total mature reviews, expressed as a percentage.

Why it matters: This is the gold standard for measuring long-term memory effectiveness. A high retention rate means your spaced repetition is working and you're truly retaining material over time.

📅 Consistency Score

What it measures: The percentage of days you studied during the selected time period, along with streak information.

How it's calculated: Number of days with at least one review divided by the total number of days in the period.

Why it matters: Regular study is one of the strongest predictors of success with spaced repetition. Even short daily sessions outperform infrequent cramming.

⚡ Efficiency Score

What it measures: How effectively you're studying, combining both accuracy and speed into a single score.

How it's calculated: A weighted blend of accuracy (60% weight) and a speed factor (40% weight). The speed factor is calculated as min(15 seconds / your average answer time, 1.0), meaning you get full speed credit at 15 seconds or less per card.

Why it matters: Efficiency captures study quality per unit of time. High accuracy with reasonable speed means you're reviewing material confidently without overthinking.

📊 Activity Summary

What it measures: Days active, current streak, longest streak, and average streak length.

How it's calculated: Streaks are counted by finding consecutive days with at least one review across your entire history.

Why it matters: Tracking streaks and active days helps you build and maintain consistent study habits. Your current streak is a powerful motivator.

📈 Key Metrics Over Time

What it measures: Weekly trends of your retention, consistency, and efficiency scores plotted on a timeline.

How it's calculated: Each data point represents a week's aggregated performance for each metric.

Why it matters: Shows your improvement trajectory over time. Upward trends confirm your study strategy is working; downward trends signal it's time to adjust.

📉 Usage Trends

What it measures: Daily study duration, number of cards reviewed, and seconds spent per card over time.

How it's calculated: Each data point is derived from your review log, aggregated by day. The average line shows your typical daily performance.

Why it matters: Helps you understand your study volume patterns and identify whether you're spending the right amount of time on reviews.

🕐 Performance by Time of Day

What it measures: Your review count, retention rate, and efficiency broken down into four time blocks: Morning (6 AM-12 PM), Afternoon (12-6 PM), Evening (6 PM-12 AM), and Night (12-6 AM).

How it's calculated: Reviews are bucketed by the hour they occurred, then retention and efficiency are computed per bucket.

Why it matters: Identifies your optimal study times. You may find you retain material better in the morning or are more efficient in the evening.

📚 Topic Performance

What it measures: Retention rate and efficiency for each of your Anki decks (topics).

How it's calculated: Reviews are grouped by their card's deck. Retention = correct / total. Efficiency uses the same weighted formula as the main score.

Why it matters: Pinpoints which subjects you're mastering and which need extra attention, so you can focus your study time where it matters most.

🏷️ Tag Performance

What it measures: Retention rate and efficiency for each of your Anki tags, with review count.

How it's calculated: Reviews are grouped by the tags on their parent note. Cards with multiple tags count toward each tag. The table shows the leaf tag name, but you can search by the full tag path (e.g., "Biology::Cells").

Why it matters: Tags often represent finer-grained topics than decks. If your cards are tagged by subject or concept, this table reveals exactly which areas need more work.

⏱️ Session Analysis

What it measures: Average and median session length, sessions per day, optimal session length, and a distribution of session durations.

How it's calculated: Sessions are detected by grouping reviews with gaps of less than 30 minutes between them. The optimal session length is the duration range with the highest retention rate.

Why it matters: Helps you plan your study schedule. Knowing your typical session length and how many sessions you do per day guides better time management.

🗓️ Weekly Heatmap

What it measures: A 7-day by 24-hour grid showing when you study, with intensity based on review count.

How it's calculated: Each cell counts the number of reviews that occurred during that day-of-week and hour combination.

Why it matters: Gives a clear visual picture of your actual study schedule, helping you identify patterns and gaps in your routine.

🔄 Retention by Interval

What it measures: Your pass rate for cards at different interval lengths (1 day, 3 days, 7 days, up to 365 days).

How it's calculated: Reviews of mature cards are bucketed by their previous interval, then the pass rate is calculated per bucket.

Why it matters: Shows how well your memory holds at different spacings. A steep drop-off at longer intervals may suggest your cards need better formulation.

👥 Cohort Comparison

What it measures: How your average study duration, cards per day, and seconds per card compare to other learners.

How it's calculated: Currently uses your own values as placeholders. Future versions will include real peer comparison data.

Why it matters: Provides context for your study habits. Understanding how you compare to peers helps set realistic goals.

💔 Lapse Analysis

What it measures: Total lapses (forgotten cards), lapse rate, and which topics/decks have the most lapses.

How it's calculated: A lapse is counted when you press "Again" (ease=1) on a review card (type=1). Lapse rate = total lapses / total reviews.

Why it matters: Identifies your weakest areas. High-lapse topics may need better mnemonics, more context, or restructured cards.

🏆 Mature Card Stats

What it measures: Statistics for your well-learned cards (interval of 21+ days): total count, retention rate, average interval, and longest interval.

How it's calculated: Queries cards where type=2 (review queue) and interval is 21+ days. Retention is from the mature review pass rate.

Why it matters: Mature cards represent your long-term knowledge base. A growing count with high retention means your learning system is effective.

🚀 Learning Velocity

What it measures: How quickly you're absorbing new material: new cards per day, graduation rate, and average time to graduate a card.

How it's calculated: New card introductions per day from your review log. Graduation rate = cards that reached review queue / total new cards introduced. Time to graduate is estimated from average learning steps.

Why it matters: Tracks your pace of learning new material. Balancing new card intake with review load is key to sustainable long-term study.
